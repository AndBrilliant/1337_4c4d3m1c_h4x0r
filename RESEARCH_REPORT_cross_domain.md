★ **J. Zak**, "Berry's phase for energy bands in solids," *Physical Review Letters* **62** (1989) 2747–2750. — Introduces the Zak phase and its quantization (0 or π) in 1D bands; the prototype of a Berry phase fixing a Z₂-valued physical parameter (electric polarization mod e).

✦ **W.-K. Tse and A. H. ### 5. Geometric means in effective-medium theory

### 6. Curvature κ = √m as a physical variable

★ **J. ★ **I. I. Satija** (2016) [arXiv:1606.09119]. — Treats Apollonian-gasket bends as the labels of CM observables (Chern numbers, gaps) in the Hofstadter problem; concretizes the √m ↔ κ identification in a CM context.

✦ **W. Helfrich**, "Elastic properties of lipid bilayers: theory and possible experiments," *Zeitschrift für Naturforschung C* **28** (1973) 693–703. — The canonical reference for Gaussian curvature K and mean curvature H as the *physical* variables of a membrane's bending energy; useful as the precedent that "curvature is a physical observable, not just a geometric convenience."

✦ **X. Gonze, J.-M. Beuken, R. ### 7. Koide formula in CM contexts / Koide via CM language

★ **J. Kocik**, "The Koide lepton mass formula and geometry of circles," arXiv:1201.2067 (2012). — **The single most relevant paper for your work**; observes that representing masses as reciprocals of disk areas (so that "curvatures [are] square roots of masses") turns Koide's identity into a Descartes-type circle relation with coefficient 2 → 2/3, which is the geometric/CM-flavored reading of Koide that you build on.

★ **R. Foot**, "A note on Koide's lepton mass relation," arXiv:hep-ph/9402242 (1994). — The geometric reinterpretation: Koide's 2/3 = cos²(θ) of the angle between the mass-square-root vector (√m_e, √m_μ, √m_τ) and the diagonal (1,1,1) — i.e. a *projection* of √m onto a symmetric C₃ axis; the same structure as crystallographic C₃-projection arguments.

★ **Y. Sumino**, "Family gauge symmetry and Koide's mass formula," *Physics Letters B* **671** (2009) 477–480 [arXiv:0812.2090]. — Embeds the Koide formula in a U(3)_family gauge theory, cancelling QED corrections at the 10²–10³ TeV scale; the most-cited published "explanation" paper. The U(3) family symmetry structure is close to flavor/orbital U(N) symmetries in CM Hubbard-type models.

★ **K. Shulga (ICEPP, University of Tokyo)**, "Charged-Lepton Koide Geometry from a Green-Dressed Compact Family Cycle," arXiv:2605.10245v1 [hep-ph] (May 11, 2026). — **Verified preprint, directly relevant to your construction**: builds an effective compact-cycle model in which (√m_e, √m_μ, √m_τ) is sampled from one real amplitude Z(φ) on an internal circle with m_a ∝ |Z(2πa/3)|², deriving δ = 2/9. This is the immediate precedent for your Berry-phase/C₃ approach and must be cited and engaged.

★ **Y. Koide**, "A fermion–boson composite model of quarks and leptons," *Physics Letters B* **120** (1983) 161–165; and "New view of quark and lepton mass hierarchy," *Physical Review D* **28** (1983) 252–254. — The original Koide papers; primary references for the empirical relation itself.

✦ **N. Li and B.-Q. Ma**, "Energy scale independence of Koide's relation for quark and lepton masses," *Physical Review D* **73** (2006) 013009 [hep-ph/0601031]. — Demonstrates that the Koide deviation parameter k is essentially RG-invariant over a wide range of energy scales — exactly the scale-free property that connects Koide to category 8.

✦ **A. Kartavtsev**, "A remark on the Koide relation for quarks," arXiv:1111.0480 (2011). — Generalizes Koide to six quarks; shows the deviation ΔK_q crosses zero between 2 GeV and M_Z under RG running, identifying a *fixed-point-like* scale at which K_q = 2/3 for quarks.

✦ **A. Rivero and A. Gsponer**, "The strange formula of Dr. Koide," arXiv:hep-ph/0505220 (2005). — Historical/bibliographical review of Koide-formula research.

## Caveats

- **Kocik (2012)** is an arXiv preprint that, as of May 2026, carries only the arXiv DOI 10.48550/arXiv.1201.2067 and no journal-publication record (NASA ADS Bibcode 2012arXiv1201.2067K lists it under "arXiv e-prints" only). It is nevertheless the most direct precedent for your κ=√m geometric reading of Koide; treat it as prior art you must cite and extend, not as a refereed-journal endorsement.
- **Shulga (2026, arXiv:2605.10245)** is a very recent preprint (May 11, 2026) and is directly competitive with your construction (it independently derives the same δ=2/9 from a compact-cycle/Green-function structure). You must (a) engage explicitly, (b) state precisely how your derivation differs (e.g. Berry-phase versus Green-dressed-cycle), and (c) credit priority where appropriate.
- The **Dykhne** citation is unambiguously *Sov. Phys. JETP* **32** (1971) 63–65 (translation by P. J. Shepherd of Zh. Eksp. Teor. Fiz. **59** (1970) 110–115); the "pp. 63–69" variant occasionally seen in secondary sources is incorrect.
- **Matheron 1967** is a French-language monograph; if your style guide requires an English-language primary source for the 2D geometric-mean theorem, cite **Dykhne 1971** alone.
- **Leutwyler's** scheme-independence statement assumes a *mass-independent* renormalization scheme (e.g. MS-bar with continuous-mass running) and the *flavor-independence* of γ_m; in EFTs with heavy-quark thresholds, ratios involving m_c, m_b, m_t are only *piecewise* RG-invariant. Be precise about which ratios you claim.
- **Sumino 2009** explains Koide via a *family gauge symmetry* with TeV-scale physics; this is a *different mechanism* than a purely geometric/symmetry-projection derivation. Cite it but distinguish your claim clearly.
- **Volovik (2014)**: the 210 GeV figure arises specifically from the doubly-degenerate case of a top-quark condensation model analyzed via the Nambu sum rule 2M₁² + 2M₂² = 4M_t²; the ³He-B analogy *motivates* the sum rule, but the 210 GeV value is from the top-condensation model itself, not from ³He-B directly. Phrase accordingly.
- **Andrade–Herrmann (2005)** *PRL* and **Satija (2016)** *EPJST* are real papers, but their *condensed-matter physics* content (foam/granular media for Andrade; Hofstadter quantum-Hall problem for Satija) is the relevant content; do not over-claim mathematical rigor about physical foams that the papers themselves do not make.
- All Berry-phase examples in the established CM literature (TKNN, Zak, Jauregui, Atala) fix quantized values (π, 2π, or π·integer/N for C_N-symmetric systems). The only published precedent for a Berry/geometric phase fixing a *non-integer rational* like 2/9 is **Shulga (2026)**; this is a genuine novelty your paper extends, not a continuation of established CM results.
