> §6 of the source report: *Curvature κ = √m as a physical variable.*
> Hook into the F-identity manuscript: §4 of the paper turns the Koide relation into a Descartes circle theorem with $\kappa_k = \sqrt{m_k}$. This subfolder gathers the CM literature in which curvature is treated as a physical observable rather than a geometric convenience.

## Tier 1 (★ — textbook statement)

- [Ashcroft & Mermin 1976](ashcroft_mermin_1976/one_pager.md) — *Solid State Physics* (Holt, Rinehart and Winston, 1976), Ch. 12. Effective mass is the inverse curvature of the band dispersion; "mass = 1/curvature" as a CM commonplace.

## Tier 1 (★ — direct geometric prior art for κ = √m)

- *(cross-ref)* Kocik 2012 — see `../koide_geometric/kocik_2012/`. The single most relevant prior paper: rewrites Koide as a Descartes circle relation with bends = √mass.

## Tier 1 (★ — integer bends labelling CM observables)

- *(cross-ref)* Satija 2016 — see `../descartes_apollonian/satija_2016/`. Apollonian-gasket integer bends label Chern numbers / gap structure in the Hofstadter problem.

## Supporting (✦)

- [Helfrich 1973](helfrich_1973/one_pager.md) — *Elastic properties of lipid bilayers,* Z. Naturforsch. C 28 (1973) 693. Gaussian curvature K and mean curvature H as the physical variables of membrane bending energy.
- [Gonze et al. 2016](gonze_2016/one_pager.md) — *Precise effective masses from DFPT,* npj Comput. Mater. 2 (2016) 16019 [arXiv:1708.05890]. Modern operational "effective mass = inverse band curvature."
