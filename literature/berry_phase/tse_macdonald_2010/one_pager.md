# Tse & MacDonald 2010 — Magneto-optical Kerr / universal Faraday effect in TI thin films

## Reference
W.-K. Tse, A. H. MacDonald, *Giant magneto-optical Kerr effect and universal Faraday effect in thin-film topological insulators*, **Phys. Rev. Lett. 105 (2010) 057401**; arXiv:1003.2260. (Viewpoint: M. Z. Hasan, *Physics* **3** (2010) 62, arXiv:1103.3410.)

§2 (Berry phase), tier ✦ — Berry-phase-protected quantization of magneto-optical response in units of the fine-structure constant $\alpha = e^2/\hbar c$.

## Beyond-abstract summary
For a thin-film 3D topological insulator with both surfaces gapped (by magnetic doping or magnetic-substrate proximity), the magneto-optical Kerr rotation angle in the low-frequency limit is universal and quantized:
$$\theta_K = \arctan\bigl(\alpha (n_t + n_b + 1)\bigr) \approx \alpha \cdot (n_t + n_b + 1),$$
where $n_t, n_b \in \mathbb{Z}$ are the Berry-Chern numbers of the top and bottom surfaces and $\alpha = e^2/\hbar c$ is the fine-structure constant. The Faraday rotation angle is similarly $\theta_F \approx \alpha \cdot \nu$ with $\nu \in \mathbb{Z}$. The construction is the cleanest CM example of a Berry-phase-protected response that sets a *dimensionless coupling* (rather than just a momentum-space invariant).

## Use in F-identity manuscript
Useful when discussing the broader theme that Berry-phase invariants can fix dimensionless physical constants in CM (here $\alpha$ explicitly). Provides indirect support for the idea that a Berry-derived rational $2/9$ (Shulga) can in principle be experimentally realized.
