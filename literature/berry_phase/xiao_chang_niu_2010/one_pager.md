## Beyond-abstract summary
The standard pedagogical review of how the Berry phase enters solid-state physics. Three foundational properties of the Berry phase make it physical: (i) gauge invariance up to multiples of $2\pi$; (ii) geometrical nature (line integral over a loop in parameter space; expressible as a surface integral of the Berry curvature); (iii) analogy to a gauge-field structure (Berry curvature ↔ magnetic field). The review unifies an enormous variety of solid-state phenomena under this single concept: ferroelectric polarization (King-Smith / Vanderbilt), quantized adiabatic transport (Thouless), the anomalous Hall effect (Karplus–Luttinger as Berry-curvature-driven), orbital magnetization, the various Hall effects (integer, anomalous, spin, valley), the semiclassical equations of motion with anomalous velocity ($\dot{\mathbf{r}} = \partial E/\partial\mathbf{k} - \dot{\mathbf{k}} \times \boldsymbol{\Omega}$), the orbital moment and dipole, magnetic Bloch bands, and the non-Abelian generalization including the Dirac and spin-Hall electron. A common thread is the semiclassical formulation of electron dynamics with Berry-curvature corrections.

## Sections / contents
- I. Introduction — properties of the Berry phase; gauge invariance, geometry, gauge-field analogy.
- II. Adiabatic transport and electric polarization — Rice–Mele model.
- III. Electron dynamics in electric fields — anomalous velocity, anomalous Hall effect.
- IV. Wave packet construction — orbital moment, anomalous thermoelectric transport.
- V. Magnetotransport in electromagnetic fields — modified DOS, Streda formula, cyclotron period.
- VI. General perturbations — deformed crystals, spin texture.
- VII. Quantization (Bohr–Sommerfeld, Wannier–Stark, de Haas–van Alphen).
- VIII. Magnetic Bloch bands — Hofstadter butterfly, semiclassical hyperorbits.
- IX. Non-Abelian formulation — spin Hall, Dirac, semiconductor electron.
- X. Outlook.

## Use in the F-identity manuscript
Cited as the canonical CM reference for "Berry phases fix observables." The F-identity paper invokes Shulga's Berry-phase derivation of $\delta = 2/9$; Xiao–Chang–Niu 2010 provides the general framework into which Shulga's compact-cycle construction fits — and the dictionary of established CM results (TKNN, Zak, anomalous Hall) that contextualize "what kind of physical parameter a Berry phase can fix." Crucial caveat for the manuscript's Discussion: every published CM example fixes a *quantized* (integer or rational-of-$\pi$) value. The novel content of Shulga (and by extension the F-identity construction's use of it) is the extension to a non-integer rational $2/9$.
