## Reference

## Beyond-abstract summary
A 500-page monograph treating superfluid $^3$He as a tabletop model of relativistic quantum field theory. The central claim: chiral fermions, Lorentz invariance, gauge fields, gravity, and topological invariants are all *emergent* phenomena in the low-energy effective theory near the Fermi-point or A-phase texture of $^3$He. The relativistic structure is not fundamental; it arises in a long-wavelength limit. The book derives — within the $^3$He framework — analogs of the chiral anomaly, the axial anomaly, baryogenesis, cosmic-string formation, quantum-vacuum energy / cosmological constant, and the topological classification of order parameters. The mathematical apparatus is the same as in HEP (Dirac equation, gauge-field action, Wess-Zumino terms), but the physical realization is solid-state. Key for the F-identity paper's positioning: Volovik's framework legitimizes the *direction* (CM mathematics applied to HEP questions) at book length, with three decades of detailed worked examples.

## Sections / contents
*(Book TOC, paraphrased)*
- Part I. Quantum fluids — superfluid order parameter, broken symmetries in $^3$He-A and $^3$He-B.
- Part II. Quantum field theory in $^3$He-like systems — Dirac fermions from Fermi points; emergent gauge fields; chiral anomaly.
- Part III. Topological defects — cosmic strings, monopoles, hedgehogs, walls; Kibble-Zurek mechanism.
- Part IV. Anomalies of chiral vacuum — axial anomaly experiment in $^3$He-A.
- Part V. Fermions on topological objects and brane physics.
- Part VI. Edge states and Andreev bound states.
- Part VII. Quantum-vacuum energy / cosmological constant from condensed-matter analogs.
