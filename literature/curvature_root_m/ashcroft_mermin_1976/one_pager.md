# Ashcroft & Mermin 1976 — Solid State Physics

## Reference
N. W. Ashcroft, N. D. Mermin, **Solid State Physics** (Holt, Rinehart and Winston, 1976; reprinted Saunders, Philadelphia, 1976), 826 pp., ISBN 0-03-083993-9. Textbook; no arXiv.

## Source
§6 (Curvature κ = √m as physical variable), tier ★ — the textbook statement of "mass = inverse band curvature."

## Beyond-abstract summary
The standard graduate-level CM textbook. Chapter 12 ("The semiclassical model of electron dynamics") gives the canonical statement of the effective mass:
$$(m^*)^{-1}_{\alpha\beta} = \hbar^{-2}\, \frac{\partial^2 E(\mathbf{k})}{\partial k_\alpha \partial k_\beta},$$
i.e. the effective mass is the inverse Hessian (curvature) of the band dispersion in $\mathbf{k}$-space. This establishes "mass ↔ inverse curvature" as a CM commonplace — the routine identification that the F-identity manuscript exploits in reverse (treating $\sqrt{m}$ as a curvature in the Descartes/Soddy sense).

## Use in F-identity manuscript
The single textbook reference for "in CM, mass is a curvature." When a referee questions the legitimacy of treating $\sqrt{m_k}$ as the bend of a circle, Ashcroft–Mermin Ch. 12 (cited alongside Kocik 2012 and Satija 2016) establishes that the mass↔curvature identification is a routine CM commonplace.
