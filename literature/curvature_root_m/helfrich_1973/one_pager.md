# Helfrich 1973 — Elastic properties of lipid bilayers

## Reference
W. Helfrich, *Elastic properties of lipid bilayers: theory and possible experiments*, **Z. Naturforsch. C 28 (1973) 693–703**. German-journal article; no arXiv (1973).

## Source
§6 (Curvature κ = √m as physical variable), tier ✦ — canonical example of "curvature as physical observable."

## Beyond-abstract summary
The foundational paper on the elastic theory of lipid-bilayer membranes. Helfrich proposes the bending-energy density
$$\mathcal{F} = \frac{1}{2}\kappa_c (2H - c_0)^2 + \bar\kappa_G K,$$
where $H = (\kappa_1 + \kappa_2)/2$ is the mean curvature, $K = \kappa_1\kappa_2$ is the Gaussian curvature, $c_0$ is the spontaneous curvature, and $\kappa_c, \bar\kappa_G$ are bending moduli. The work establishes that Gaussian and mean curvature are the *physical* observables of a membrane (not merely geometric conveniences), with bending energies $\sim 10$–$20\, k_B T$ measurable experimentally. The Helfrich Hamiltonian remains the foundation of membrane physics 50 years later.
