## Beyond-abstract summary
Three-page note (with one earlier preprint version typeset alongside) reading the Koide identity $m_e + m_\mu + m_\tau = \tfrac{2}{3}(\sqrt{m_e}+\sqrt{m_\mu}+\sqrt{m_\tau})^2$ as a special case of a generalized Descartes circle theorem. The key move is identifying masses with reciprocals of disk areas, so that *curvatures (bends) are square roots of masses*. The classical Descartes identity (1654) for four mutually tangent circles, $(a+b+c+d)^2 = 2(a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2)$, has coefficient 2 on the right-hand side; Kocik's generalized circle formula (Theorem II.2, from his earlier arXiv:0706.0372) replaces this with $p/(3p-1)$ where $p = \cos\varphi$ is the (common) pairwise intersection angle, recovering Descartes at $p=1$ and Koide at $p=2/3$ ($\varphi \approx 48.2^\circ$). The fourth "particle" is a straight line ($b_4=0$). A higher-dimensional version $p = 2/(2n-1)$ is sketched as a candidate for generalizations to more particles.

## Sections / contents
- I. Introduction — Koide identity, tau prediction history.
- II. From Descartes to Koide — Descartes circle theorem; generalized circle formula; the $p=2/3$ corollary; arccos(2/3) ≈ 48.2°.
- III. Conclusions — (1) lepton-segment 1D version, (2) neutrino sector with negative $\sqrt{m_1}$ (Brannen), (3) quark sector with $p=2/9$ giving $\varphi \approx 77.2^\circ$, (4) coda noting that the analogy "may turn out to be merely superficial."

## Use in the F-identity manuscript
Cited as `Kocik2012` in Sec. 4 ("Why Q=2/3") for the curvature reading $\kappa_k = \sqrt{m_k}$. The F-identity paper extends Kocik in two structural ways: (i) it introduces the **F-identity** $F = \sum\sqrt{m_k} - \sqrt{\sum m_k}$ — Kocik does not write this object — and proves $F^2$ reduces to the Soddy companion *only at* $Q=2/3$; (ii) it embeds the construction in $C_3 \times Z_2$ symmetry, giving a derivation of $Q=2/3$ that Kocik does not provide. The Koide–Soddy cascade ratio $R_K = 1/(\sqrt{3/2}-1)^2 = 19.798$ in Eq. (8) of the F-identity paper is implicit in Kocik's $\varphi = \arccos(2/3)$ geometry but not written in his form.
