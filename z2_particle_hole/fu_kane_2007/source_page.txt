In three dimensions there are four Z_2 invariants, which distinguish the ordinary insulator from &#34;weak&#34; and &#34;strong&#34; topological insulators. These phases are characterized by the presence of gapless surface (or edge) states. In the 2D quantum spin Hall phase and the 3D strong topological insulator these states are robust and are insensitive to weak disorder and interactions. In this paper we show that the presence of inversion symmetry greatly simplifies the problem of evaluating the Z_2 invariants. We show that the invariants can be determined from the knowledge of the parity of the occupied Bloch wavefunctions at the time reversal invariant points in the Brillouin zone. This paper also includes an expanded discussion of our formulation of the topological insulators in both two and three dimensions, as well as implications for experiments.
